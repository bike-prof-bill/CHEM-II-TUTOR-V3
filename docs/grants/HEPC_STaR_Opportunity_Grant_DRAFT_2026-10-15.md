## 1. Event or Initiative Summary (300 words; draft 288)

General Chemistry II is a gate: a student who cannot say which way an equilibrium shifts rarely goes on in nursing,
engineering, pharmacy or the sciences. Marshall University has built a tutor for this course that works the way a good office
hour works. Every problem opens with a short scene in which an instrument shows something odd (a syringe of water boiling cold; a
counter clicking over a beaker that looks still), and the student must say what the particles are doing before any number
appears. The tutor never gives an answer; it checks each step, recognises the common wrong turns by name, asks one question aimed
at each, and records every turn. Two chapters, nine simulations and more than ninety problems are built; faculty are reviewing
it now.

We propose a two-part statewide initiative in 2027.

**Part 1, the Scenario Workshop (spring).** A one-day hybrid workshop, in person at Marshall and online, for General Chemistry
instructors from West Virginia's public four-year institutions and community and technical colleges, and for dual-credit and AP
chemistry teachers. Participants try the tutor as students, then write scenarios set in their own places: a mine ventilation fan, a
Kanawha Valley chlor-alkali plant, a sugar camp evaporator. Scenarios that pass the chemistry check are built into the tutor
with the author's name and institution.

**Part 2, the Student Challenge (fall).** Two weeks in which General Chemistry II students at every participating institution
use the tutor on the equilibrium chapter, with participation awards drawn at each institution.

Audience: about [40] instructors and [600 to 1,000] students across the state in the first year. Goal: a tutor that speaks in
West Virginia's own scenes, written by its own teachers, used in every region.

**Geographic reach.** The tutor is a web page and a spreadsheet; it needs a browser and nothing else, so distance is no barrier
to use. The workshop is hybrid for the same reason: an instructor in Keyser or Welch joins online with the same materials, the
same tutor access and the same chance to have a scenario adopted as one who drives to Huntington. In-person attendance is for those who can come,
with travel stipends beyond [100] miles.

**Target audiences.** Three groups. (1) General Chemistry instructors at West Virginia's public four-year institutions [list:

teach the same equilibrium and vapor-pressure content a year earlier and can bring the tutor to students before they choose a
major. (3) General Chemistry II students at participating institutions, who use the tutor in the fall challenge.

**Recruitment.** Instructors are reached through the West Virginia Academy of Science [confirm], the state's chemistry

**Engagement that lasts.** A scenario written by a Concord instructor and used by a Shepherd student is a connection that

**Why this is statewide.** The content is the standard General Chemistry II syllabus (OpenStax Chemistry 2e [confirm its

## 3. Contribution to STEM Education and Workforce Development (300 words; draft 279)

**STEM education.** The tutor does the one thing a lecture cannot: it makes every student explain, in words, what the particles
are doing, and it will not move on until they do. The explain-first design follows the learning science the project rests on
(predict, observe, explain; self-explanation; immediate, targeted feedback) and the tutor's log makes the effect measurable, by
student and by concept: which wrong turns are common at which institution, and whether they fall over a semester. Instructors in
the workshop leave with a tool they can use the next week and with the data it produces about their own students.

**Interest and participation in STEM.** General Chemistry II is where many West Virginia students leave science, not because
they cannot do it but because no one had time to find out exactly where they stopped understanding. A tutor that finds that
point, asks one question, and records it, is a retention tool as much as a teaching tool. Scenes set in the state's own
industries (chemicals in the Kanawha Valley, natural gas, coal, timber and maple, health care) show students that the chemistry
they are learning is the chemistry of jobs within an hour's drive.

**The value of STEM in West Virginia.** The initiative produces a public, growing, West Virginia-authored set of chemistry
scenarios, each tied to a real place and a real instrument reading, usable by any teacher in the state. It is a small
demonstration that the state can build its own educational technology, grounded in its own places, rather than buy it.

**Workforce.** The students are the state's future nurses, pharmacists, engineers, chemists and teachers. The instructors who
write scenarios gain a method they can apply to any course. [Add: any connection to Marshall's teacher-preparation program or to
a state workforce initiative you can name.]
