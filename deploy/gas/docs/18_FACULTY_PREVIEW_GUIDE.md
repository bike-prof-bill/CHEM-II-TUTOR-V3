# Trying the Gen Chem II tutor: a guide for faculty reviewers (preview build, Oct 2026)

This is a preview of a tutor built for CHM 212. It is not yet in front of students. You are looking at the first two
chapters it covers: Chapter 10 (vapor pressure, the Clausius-Clapeyron relation) and Chapter 13 (equilibrium: which way, how
far, and what a change does). Everything you type is recorded in a log sheet under "preview:" and your name, so that your
comments can be matched to what the tutor did.

## What the tutor is trying to do
Every problem starts with a short scene and a question about what the particles are doing. The numerical problem stays hidden
until the student gives that account. Then the student picks the relation from an unnamed list, finds each number in order, says
what the result means, and ends with one sentence about what was hardest. In Chapter 13 the student also predicts which way an
equilibrium moves before any table, and meets two changes to the mixture at the end. The tutor never gives an answer; when it
recognises a known wrong turn it asks one question aimed at that turn. Two wrong tries on one step send the student to the
course notebook; two more park the step and the run is recorded without credit.

## How to drive it
- **Your name** in the box at the top left. **Archetype** picks the chapter; **scenario** and **kind** narrow the problem; leave
  them blank for a random one. **Rung** is yours alone: *Worked* shows the tutor's worked problem with the steps shown, *Faded*
  is the normal student setting, *Independent* is the exam setting (credit only there).
- Press **New problem**. Answer in plain English and numbers; units matter.
- Pictures, two short videos and simulations open on the left when the scene calls for them. Move the probe; the log records it.
  **All pictures and simulations** in the yellow bar opens a page with every one of them, so you can look without walking a problem.
- Add `?debug=1` to the page address to see the tutor's raw reply beneath each message.

## Things worth trying (ten minutes)
1. Give a vague account ("it's about equilibrium") and watch the problem stay hidden; then a real one.
2. Pick a wrong relation on purpose, twice, and see the notebook card.
3. On a Chapter 13 problem, type Q upside down (reactants over products) and see the question you get.
4. In the Silent Beaker, after the common ion is added, predict "forward" and see the question.
5. Finish one problem clean at Independent and read the closing message.

## How to comment
Type in the box in the yellow bar and press **Log comment**, at any moment: it goes into the log beside the tutor's last reply,
the problem and the step you were on, so a comment like "that question would lose a weak student" can be read with exactly
what the tutor said. Comment as often as you like. **Email the instructor** is there for anything longer. Comments about the
chemistry, the wording, or what a weak student would make of a step are the most useful. The instructor reads every one.
