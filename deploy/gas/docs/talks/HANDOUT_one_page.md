# The Guarded Tutor (one page)

**What it is.** A free tutor for General Chemistry II that will not hand students the answer. Every problem opens with a scene and
asks what the particles are doing before any number appears. The student picks the relation from an unnamed list, finds each number
in order with its units, says which way an equilibrium goes and what a change does, says what the result means, and ends with one
sentence on what was hardest. Known wrong turns are recognised by name and met with one question written by the instructor. Every
turn is recorded.

**Why "guarded".** Bastani et al. (2025, PNAS) randomised about a thousand students among no AI, an unguarded chatbot, and the same
model held to hints and questions. The unguarded chatbot raised practice scores and lowered scores on a later exam taken without it;
the guarded version kept the gain and removed the loss. [Confirm the three figures: +48 %, −17 %, +127 %.]

**How it is built.** A checking machine that knows no chemistry (numbers, units, equations by substitution, tables cell by cell, the
two-try ladder to the course notebook, the log) · one chemistry file per chapter (every right value from the textbook's constants,
every known wrong turn as a value with a name, every question the instructor's) · a language model allowed one sentence after the
machine has ruled, overruled by a second reader if it strays. Built by the instructor with AI partners in named roles: architect,
constructor, scenario writers, simulation builder, analysis; the instructor owns every teaching decision; nothing marked DRAFT
reaches a student; every number is re-derived by a test.

**Where it stands (Oct 2026).** V2 in CHM 212 since 18 September, collecting data. V3: chapter 10 (vapor pressure) and chapter 13
(equilibrium: five scenarios, 66 problems, nine simulations) live for faculty review; chapter 14 (acids and bases, four archetypes)
built and awaiting its scenarios.
